The Haveli

A merchant's house, given back its rooms one arch at a time.

Reth Mahal was built in 1626 by a family of opium and spice traders on the old caravan road to Sindh. It fell quiet for three decades until 2011, when the carved sandstone jharokhas were uncovered under drifted sand and the restoration began — stone by stone, using the same quarry the original masons used, eleven kilometres north.

Every jaali lattice you see cut into a window or a railing here is hand-chiselled by the same family of stone-carvers who worked on the original haveli six generations ago. Nothing is reproduced from a catalogue.

A Day at Reth Mahal

Follow the sun across the courtyard — it sets the whole day's rhythm here.

The haveli was built around the sun, not the clock: rooms face east for the cool morning light, the courtyard well shades the afternoon, and the rooftop catches every desert sunset. Move through the hours below.

Beyond the Haveli

What guests actually spend their afternoons on.

Sunset dune safari

A 40-minute camel ride into the Sam dunes for sunset, followed by a folk music set around a fire — arranged privately, not with the tour-bus crowd.

Stone-carving workshop

An afternoon with the resident carving family, learning the jaali lattice technique on offcuts of the same sandstone the haveli is built from.

Rooftop stargazing

A telescope, a local astronomer, and near-zero light pollution — the desert sky here shows the Milky Way clearly most nights of the year.
